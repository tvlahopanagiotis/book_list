▶ What this list says about my taste

This list was built by scoring 111 books against a taste profile derived from 56 Goodreads ratings. The profile shows a consistent preference for books where the author has a real argument, made without padding — high scores go to intellectual non-fiction (Kahneman, Taleb, de Mesquita), literary fiction with a thesis (Dostoevsky, Ishiguro, Houellebecq), and political history; low scores to comfort reads, motivational self-help, and anything that trades rigour for accessibility. Two preoccupations run through the highest-rated titles: how power and institutions actually work beneath the surface, and moral complexity in fiction with no clean resolution.

A few things about how to read the scores. They sometimes drop on reflection: a book that impresses on first read but leaves little behind loses ground over time. Contemporary critical reception has proved a poor predictor — the lowest-scoring books are disproportionately recent literary fiction that arrived with strong reviews. Audiobook suitability is tracked separately from taste, because the highest-scoring books tend to be the worst suited to listening: dense argument does not survive the format the way narrative does.

There are limits worth naming. The profile is built almost entirely from Western European and Anglophone reading, which means predictions for Japanese, Central European, or Nordic fiction are extrapolated rather than grounded in actual ratings. The to-read list also frequently contradicts the profile it claims to express: a significant number of books scoring below 40 were added on impulse, based on a premise or a cover. The list is as much a record of what seemed interesting in a given moment as it is a map of genuine taste.
